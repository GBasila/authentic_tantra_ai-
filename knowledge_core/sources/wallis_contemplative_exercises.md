# Contemplative Exercises · Near Enemies of the Truth

**Book:** *Near Enemies of the Truth* by Christopher (Hareesh) Wallis
**Compiled by:** Виктория · reference index for personal practice
**Note:** Below is a paraphrased index in my own words, showing the intent and structure of each exercise so you can find and use them quickly. For the exact wording, refer to the book. Chapter numbers follow the "Near Enemy" numbering Wallis uses.

---

## 1. Near Enemy #2 · *Speak Your Truth*
### Exercise: What Is the Truth of My Experience?

Sit and breathe more slowly than usual. Set aside all interpretations, opinions, and any sense that something urgent needs to be resolved. Bring to mind a situation that has been challenging, and instead of naming or labeling it, simply notice the sensations it produces in the body. Meet those sensations with compassion. Then ask yourself what you actually need or long for underneath the situation, without judging that need. The truth Wallis points to here is not universal but experiential: it lives inside your own feelings, wants, and values. The commitment at the end of the exercise is to speak from that inner truth only when someone is genuinely open to receiving it.

## 2. Near Enemy #4 · *Be in the Moment*
### Exercise: Opening to Natural Harmony

Settle, breathe, and let attention rest on bodily sensations with curiosity but without labeling. Gradually widen the field of attention to include everything at once — sensations, sounds, colors, thoughts — until the whole of experience becomes one soft field of energy (staying alert, not sleepy). Then move to a three-center practice: soften the low belly and hold there a willingness to be with all somatic sensation; soften the heart and hold there a willingness to be with any emotion or thought; open the head with a sense of transparency and hold there a willingness to be with sounds, colors, and smells of the environment. Finally, feel these three centers operating in harmony with each other and with the surroundings.

---

## 3. Near Enemy #5 · *Listen to Your Heart*
### Exercise: Feeling the Pull of Innate Intuition

Used when a decision is fully understood mentally but the direction is unclear. First, meditate briefly to become calm and centered. Then choose two to four real options and mentally place each one at a different spatial position around you (in front, behind, left, right). Take a moment to feel the atmosphere of each option rather than analyzing its facts. Return to center, breathe, and release worry about being "right." Notice whether a gentle pull, warmth, or brightening arises in one direction. That subtle inclination is what Wallis calls *pratibhā* — the innate intuition that senses the pattern of life. If you want confidence, repeat over several days, rotating which option sits where, so that spatial bias averages out.

## 4. Near Enemy #6 · *Love Yourself*
### Exercise: Your Innate Value

Sit, breathe, relax. Bring attention to the raw sensation of the body without labels or storylines about it. When thoughts arise, quietly set them aside and return to sensation. Then notice not the body but the simple background feeling of being *you* — the sense of your own existence that has been continuous since childhood, prior to any self-descriptions. Rest inside that plain "I am" feeling and notice its quiet, sweet, innocent quality. Wallis frames this presence as your innate value: it can't be proved but can be directly sensed. The invitation is to relax into the recognition that, prior to any thought about yourself, you already exist perfectly.

## 5. Near Enemy #8 · *Everything Happens for the Best*
### Exercise: Finding the Blessing

A three-stage practice, done in sequence (each stage can be held for days before advancing).

**Stage 1 — the still point.** Locate the somatic center roughly midway between the pelvic floor and the crown, and roughly midway between front and back of the body (often just under the heart). Rest attention there. Notice this point is unmoving while experience swirls around it. Breathe and rest.

**Stage 2 — digesting emotions.** From center, turn toward an undigested emotion connected to a hard situation. Feel the emotion in raw form without the storyline. Once fully felt, draw it into the center and let it dissolve into your pure being. Repeat with each new wave. Notice the relief that follows.

**Stage 3 — opening to the blessing.** From a state of unknowing, ask your inner wisdom what blessing this difficult situation carries. Don't reach for the answer with the mind. Let it arrive slowly, perhaps first as feeling before words.

## 6. Near Enemy #9 · *Find Your Soul's Purpose*
### Exercise: Sensing Natural Presence

Settle, relax, breathe. Notice your thoughts. Notice your feelings. Notice everything you happen to notice. Then turn attention to the awareness itself that is doing all the noticing. Wallis argues that your very being is made of this awareness — everything else in experience comes and goes, but awareness remains. When beingness recognizes itself as awareness, no subject stands opposite an object; there is simply being-awareness-presence. Rest inside that for a while and enjoy it as the constant ground underneath all changing content.

## 7. Near Enemy #11 · *You Can Choose How to Respond*
### Exercise: Reposing in Awareness-Presence

A deepening of the previous exercise. Settle, breathe, and notice natural presence. Notice you *are* awareness itself — the perceiving that happens before the mind reacts. Let awareness rest inside itself. You cannot see what you are (you are the seeing), but you can *be* what you are. Then notice that awareness is not separate from what it perceives. Sound is awareness in the form of vibration. Sensation is awareness in the form of touch. Thought is awareness in the form of thought. There is no hearer separate from hearing, no thinker separate from thinking. Once verified in direct experience, this allows you to rest as awareness-presence no matter what content is arising in the field of experience.

## 8. Near Enemy #12 · *Negative Energy*
### Exercise: Being with "Negative" Emotions

Practice this when a difficult emotion is present. Settle, breathe, and feel whatever you are feeling in full. Then strip away the interpretation: drop the reason-story for it, drop even the label ("anger," "sadness," "shame"). Feel the sensation without any narrative. Notice how the emotion, undressed of story, becomes pure intensity. Move deeper: *become* the intensity. Notice how it now shows up as pure aliveness. Let that intense aliveness move — a minute of vocal sound, shaking, dancing, whatever comes. Then return to stillness. Feel the quiet aliveness suffusing your being and, if it comes naturally, regard it as sacred. Wallis calls this sacred aliveness. Exhale.

---

## 9. Near Enemy #15 · *All Paths Lead to the Same Goal*
### Exercise: Clarify Your Aspiration

A journaling contemplation. Sit quietly with a notebook. Ask: what am I actually seeking to actualize through spiritual practice? What do I want? What result do I envision? Wallis specifically disallows answers like "enlightenment" or "liberation" unless you can say precisely what those words mean to you experientially. Write one or two sentences describing what you're aiming to embody more fully — ideally something you've already had a small taste of. An acceptable exception is "I want to realize what is most deeply true, whatever that turns out to be." Expect to revise this articulation every few months as direct experience sharpens what the path can and cannot deliver.

## 10. Near Enemies #16 & #17 · *The Universe Is Giving Me a Sign* / *Go With the Flow*
### Exercise: The Currents of Life

Take long slow breaths until you feel settled. Then let awareness rest on your whole life situation as it currently is, without adding judgments or verbal commentary. From this open ground, sense whether there are currents already moving in your life that you have been overlooking. It can help to ask: what do I already know to be true that I haven't admitted to myself? Do not look for a verbal answer — words may follow, but the practice is to sense what is present in perception beyond the five senses. This awareness arrives slowly. Be patient.

## 11. Chapter · *Ego*
### Exercise: Where Is the Self?

Settle, breathe. First, notice the thoughts you have about the person you assume yourself to be — name, story, roles. Then notice the space of awareness inside which those thoughts arise. That space itself has no name, no gender, no identity. Turn attention to the mass of sensation you call the body and notice it too is prior to your thoughts about it. Notice that a simple beingness suffuses both sensation and thought equally. When a thought about being a separate self arises, notice beingness is suffusing that thought to the same degree as any other content. Since your beingness underlies all experience equally, you are no more your self-thoughts than you are any other passing content. Rest as this simple beingness.

---

## 12. Chapter · *Surrender*
### Exercise: Melting Resistance into Openness

Sit and breathe. Notice what part of today's experience you are resisting — subtly or obviously. Surround the resistance with gentle openness, letting it be as it is. If possible, surround it with love. Slowly let the knot of resistance unravel. Tears, a deep breath, or a sigh may come. As the body opens, become the openness itself. Breathe as pure openness. Let whatever wants to move through you move through, and stay as the openness through which the energy passes. There is nothing to grab and nothing to push away — openness does neither. From here, Wallis notes, you may see the beauty of what is, feel gratitude, or laugh at yourself. Rest as quiet openness. Let it be a blessing.

## Notes for practice

- These exercises are Wallis's, paraphrased here so this file can serve as your working index. When you sit with any of them for real, the original wording in the book is worth going back to — his phrasing is precise and often opens the exercise further.
- Several exercises share a common architecture: settle → notice sensation without story → notice the awareness noticing → rest as that awareness. This is the Kaula-adjacent recognition-based (*pratyabhijñā*) approach Wallis is teaching throughout the book.
- The exercises can be layered. Wallis explicitly links #7 (Reposing in Awareness-Presence) back to #6 (Sensing Natural Presence). #5 (Finding the Blessing) is meant to be practiced in stages across days or weeks.
- For workshop use, exercise #4 (Your Innate Value), #8 (Being with "Negative" Emotions), and #12 (Melting Resistance into Openness) translate especially well into guided practice for groups.
